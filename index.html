A 63-year test of Sharpe's CAPM (1964)

Does more risk mean more return?

The CAPM says each extra unit of market risk, beta, should earn the full market premium. We tested it on 759 months of US stocks, then built a monitor that reads the market every morning.

Short answer

Risk pays, but only about half as much as the theory says.

How the signals work
  • Market regime (S&P 500): Risk-on above its 200-day average and less than 10% below its 12-month high; Caution below its 200-day average; Correction 10 to 20% below its high; Bear market more than 20% below.
  • Style (1-year beta): Defensive below 0.9, Aggressive above 1.1, as in Sharpe (1964, p. 441-442).
  • Risk-on: follow the trend. Overweight a sector above its 200-day average that beat the market over 3 months.
  • Any other regime: protect the portfolio. Favour defensive sectors, which held up best in past recessions.

Rule-based signals built for a class project. Not investment advice.

The research

The test: 10 portfolios sorted by beta

US stocks are sorted into 10 portfolios, from the lowest beta (D1) to the highest (D10). If the CAPM is right, their returns sit on the dashed line. They don't: the actual line is half as steep and starts well above zero.

01

Low beta earns too much. D1 beats its CAPM prediction by 2.4% a year (t = 2.48, significant).

02

High beta earns too little. D10 has 2.7 times the beta of D1, but earns only about 2 points more.

03

Joint test. GRS = 1.78, p = 0.061: the 10 alphas are jointly non-zero at the 10% level, not at 5%.

Data: Kenneth R. French Data Library, value-weighted portfolios, 1963-07 to 2026-09. Same results in R, Databricks SQL and Python.

Defensive vs aggressive

Which sectors hold up in recessions?

In the 85 months of NBER recessions, the market lost 7.4% a year over the risk-free rate. Retail, healthcare and consumer staples held up; energy and manufacturing fell hardest. This is Sharpe's split between "defensive" and "aggressive" assets.

Behind the scenes

How it's built

Directed step by step with Claude Code, from raw CSV files to this page.

  1. Data

    Kenneth French Data Library (monthly, since 1963) and Yahoo Finance (daily sector ETFs).

  2. Python

    pandas and statsmodels: CAPM regressions, OLS diagnostics, Newey-West, GRS, Fama-MacBeth.

  3. Databricks SQL

    Bronze, silver and gold tables. The CAPM beta is computed in pure SQL as covariance / variance.

  4. Power BI

    A 3-page dashboard connected to the gold tables.

  5. This website

    A Windows scheduled task runs the pipeline every trading morning and republishes the data here.

So, does risk pay?

Yes, but not the way Sharpe predicted. The reward for beta is real but flat, low-beta stocks are underpriced, and defensive sectors protect investors when the economy turns. These gaps point to the assumptions Sharpe himself called "highly restrictive": unlimited borrowing at the risk-free rate and identical investors.
